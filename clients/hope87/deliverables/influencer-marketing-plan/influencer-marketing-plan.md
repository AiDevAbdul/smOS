# HOPE'87 — Influencer & Community-Voice Marketing Plan

**Client:** HOPE'87 (est. 1987, humanitarian & development NGO, represented in 9 countries)
**Prepared:** 2026-07-31 · **Horizon:** first 90 days (Week 1 = week of Aug 3, 2026)
**Donor audience:** Pakistani diaspora in Austria + Germany, ages 25–55, all genders
**Cause focus:** skills-development & livelihood programs (vocational training → employment/self-employment)
**Voice:** warm, trustworthy, community-rooted, humble-but-credible; storytelling over statistics; soft CTAs
**Currency/timezone:** EUR · Europe/Vienna

**Grounding:** `clients/hope87/profile.json`, `prospects/hope87/synthesis.json` (pre-audit 26/100), and aligned to
`deliverables/3-month-roadmap/roadmap-and-weekly-plan.md` (influencer workstream weeks 2, 3, 6, 8, 11, 12) and
`deliverables/content-production-plan/content-and-creative-plan.md` (rebrand kit + Template A/B/C system).

## Why influencers matter disproportionately here

The starting position from the audit sets the strategy, not generic best practice:

- **Owned reach is tiny.** 289 Instagram followers and 2,696 Facebook likes (18 "talking about this"). HOPE'87 cannot reach the AT/DE diaspora at scale on its own accounts in 90 days. Borrowed audiences are the only fast reach available.
- **Paid reach is starting from literally zero.** 0 ads ever run in AT/DE, against Islamic Relief Worldwide's ~104 ads in 90 days (~35/month) in the same two countries. Month 1 paid is capped at €20–30/day awareness (per the roadmap), so paid cannot carry reach either.
- **The one real asset is trust, and trust transfers through people, not ads.** The profile's own "what worked" is community trust. For a donation decision inside a diaspora community, a known community voice vouching is worth more than impressions — money follows social proof from within the group, especially where donors are choosing between competing appeals in their feed.
- **The competitor is beatable on this axis specifically.** Islamic Relief Worldwide out-runs HOPE'87 on paid ad volume by ~35:1. It does not out-run HOPE'87 on *local Austrian/German Pakistani-community relationships* — that is a defensible, non-budget-dependent channel.

**Framing to use with the client:** this is not "influencer marketing" in the brand-deal sense. It is **community-voice amplification** — the diaspora equivalent of a mosque announcement or a community-dinner appeal, executed on Instagram and Facebook. Budget expectations should be set accordingly (mostly non-cash), and so should tone (invitation to co-own a cause, never a rate-card negotiation).

## 1. Influencer Archetype Targeting

Five archetypes, ranked. The ranking is by **expected donated euros per hour of relationship effort** for *this* donor base — not by follower count. For a €50–150 average diaspora donation decision, trust dominates reach.

### Ranking summary

| # | Archetype | Typical size | Trust with this donor base | Reach | Ask difficulty | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pakistani cultural/community **organization leaders** (AT/DE) | 500–5,000 (org page + personal network) | **Very high** — vouching is institutional | Low–medium, but *exactly* the right people | Low (mission-aligned, no fee expected) | **Tier 1 — start here** |
| 2 | Diaspora **community micro-influencers** (Pakistani-Austrian / Pakistani-German everyday creators) | 3k–25k | **High** — peer proof from inside the group | Medium | Low–medium (gifted/ambassador realistic) | **Tier 1** |
| 3 | **Mosque / community-centre-affiliated voices** (imams, youth-group leads, women's-circle organisers) | Offline congregation 200–2,000; small online | **Highest per-person**, but denominationally bounded | Low online, high offline | Medium (requires in-person respect + client presence) | **Tier 2 — high value, handle carefully** |
| 4 | Diaspora **content creators** (food / culture / faith-adjacent lifestyle) | 20k–150k | Medium — audience is diaspora but relationship is entertainment, not trust-in-causes | **Highest** | Medium–high (may quote a fee) | **Tier 2 — use for reach bursts** |
| 5 | **Second-generation diaspora youth creators** (bilingual DE/EN + Urdu/Punjabi, culture-bridging) | 5k–60k | Medium-high with under-35s, lower with the 45–55 donor core | Medium–high | Low (cause-motivated, agency-fluent) | **Tier 2 — the long game** |

**Read of that table:** Tiers 1 gets you *donations*; Tier 2 gets you *reach and future donors*. With 289 IG followers, HOPE'87 needs both — but if Month 1 forces a choice, work the organizations and micro-influencers first, because they convert without paid support and they don't need HOPE'87's own account to look big.

### 1.1 Pakistani cultural/community organization leaders — Tier 1, highest trust

**Who:** chairpersons, general secretaries, and event organisers of Pakistani associations, cultural societies, business/professional networks, alumni chapters, and Urdu-language cultural circles in Austria and Germany. In Austria the density is Vienna-first (with Graz and Linz secondary); in Germany the diaspora clusters are Frankfurt/Offenbach, Hamburg, Berlin, Cologne/Düsseldorf, Munich, and the Ruhr area.

**Why they rank #1:** when a community-association chair posts "I have known HOPE'87's work for years — please support this," it functions as institutional vetting. For a 25–55 diaspora donor deciding whether an NGO is legitimate, that single sentence resolves the exact objection paid ads cannot: *is this real, and does someone I know stand behind it?* HOPE'87's stated USP (est. 1987, 9 countries, decades of on-the-ground credibility) is precisely the kind of claim these people can *confirm* rather than merely repeat.

**Trust vs reach:** low reach individually (a few hundred to a few thousand, often a mix of a Facebook group, a WhatsApp broadcast list, and a personal profile) but near-zero wastage — these audiences *are* the target segment, at the right ages, in the right countries.

**What to ask for:** a personal-voice post/story, an intro to their WhatsApp community list, a slot in their next event (a 3-minute mic moment plus a QR code to the donation page), and referrals to two other community voices. Never a fee.

**Note on the Facebook skew:** this archetype is disproportionately active on **Facebook**, not Instagram — which matches where HOPE'87's own equity sits (2,696 FB likes vs 289 IG followers). Do not force Instagram-first collabs on Tier 1; let them post where their community actually reads them.

### 1.2 Diaspora community micro-influencers — Tier 1, best trust/reach ratio

**Who:** Pakistani-Austrian and Pakistani-German creators in the 3k–25k range whose content is *about diaspora life* rather than a vertical: bilingual daily-life posts, family/parenting, Vienna/Berlin/Frankfurt city content in Urdu-inflected German or English, "growing up Pakistani in Europe" commentary, wedding/eid/community-event coverage.

**Why they rank #2:** micro-accounts in a tight ethnic-community niche behave like friends, not media. Comment sections are conversational, DMs get answered, and followers overlap heavily with the actual donor pool. Their endorsement carries near-Tier-1 trust at 5–20× the reach.

**Trust vs reach:** the best balance in the whole list. This is where the bulk of partnership slots should go.

**Screen for:** genuine AT/DE residence (not Pakistan-based accounts with diaspora followers), comment-section quality over follower count (real conversations vs emoji spam), an audience skewing 25–45, no prior involvement in donation drives that ended in public disputes, and no political/party affiliation that would make HOPE'87 look partisan.

### 1.3 Mosque and community-centre-affiliated voices — Tier 2 by reach, Tier 1 by conversion

**Who:** imams and khateebs at Urdu-language mosques, youth-group leaders, women's-circle organisers, weekend-Urdu-school teachers, and community-centre committee members.

**Why they matter:** charitable giving in this community has a strong faith-linked rhythm (Ramadan/Zakat, Qurbani, Sadaqah). A mosque-affiliated voice endorsing a *skills-and-livelihood* cause reframes the donation as "helping someone earn for themselves" — theologically and culturally resonant, and directly aligned with HOPE'87's actual programs rather than generic relief.

**Handle with care — three real constraints, flagged rather than glossed:**
1. **Denominational boundaries are real.** The Pakistani diaspora in AT/DE spans multiple schools and movements, and some are in open tension with each other. Publicly partnering with one mosque network can cost credibility with another. **Decision required from the client, not the agency:** either (a) stay institutionally neutral and work with individuals in a personal capacity, or (b) deliberately pick one network and accept the trade-off. Do not let the agency make this call by accident through outreach order.
2. **These are offline-first relationships.** Cold DMs largely fail. This archetype requires a HOPE'87 representative physically attending, being introduced, and asking in person. That means it is **client-led with agency support**, not agency-executed — budget the client's time honestly.
3. **Approval is often committee-based.** Expect 3–6 weeks from first conversation to a yes. Start early (Week 3), expect payoff in Month 2–3, not Month 1.

**What to ask for:** an announcement/QR code at a community event or after prayers, a mention in the community newsletter or WhatsApp group, and — the highest-value version — permission to run a **skills-program appeal moment** during a seasonal giving window.

### 1.4 Diaspora content creators (food / culture / faith-adjacent lifestyle) — Tier 2, reach engine

**Who:** 20k–150k accounts with a clear content vertical and a majority-diaspora audience: desi food in Vienna/Berlin, Pakistani recipe creators posting in Urdu/German, culture-and-heritage accounts, modest-fashion creators, faith-adjacent lifestyle accounts.

**Why Tier 2 not Tier 1:** the relationship between a food creator and their audience is entertainment-based. Followers trust the *recipe*, not necessarily the cause recommendation. Conversion per impression is materially lower than Tier 1 — but total reach is far higher, and for an account with 289 IG followers, raw reach has real strategic value (it builds the retargetable audience the Month-2+ paid campaigns need).

**Best use:** reach bursts tied to a moment (a seasonal appeal, a campaign launch, a match-challenge), not always-on ambassadorship. Also the archetype most likely to quote a fee — see §2.

**Screen for:** brand-safety on prior sponsorships (gambling, dubious financial products, and "get rich" schemes are common in this creator segment and would contaminate an NGO's trust position), and audience-geography — plenty of large "desi" accounts have Pakistan- or UK-majority audiences, which is wasted reach for AT/DE donation targeting. Ask for an insights screenshot of top countries and age split before agreeing to anything.

### 1.5 Second-generation diaspora youth creators — Tier 2, strategic long game

**Who:** 18–30-year-old creators born or raised in Austria/Germany, fluent in German plus Urdu/Punjabi, whose content explicitly bridges the two cultures — code-switching humour, "translating my parents" content, identity commentary, student/early-career life.

**Why they're strategically valuable despite weaker immediate conversion:** they are the *only* archetype that speaks natively to the German-language half of the bilingual EN/DE strategy while remaining culturally inside the community. They also solve a structural problem: HOPE'87's donor core (45–55) will age out, and the 25–35 segment gives through different mechanics (Instagram-native, mobile, card-not-bank-transfer, cause-over-institution). Building these relationships in Q1 is what makes the 2027 donor base exist.

**Best use:** the German-language creative half, Story takeovers, and "match my donation" challenges — formats where energy and personality outperform gravitas.

**Honest caveat:** expect lower euros-per-post than Tier 1 in the first 90 days. Justify these partnerships as audience-building and DE-language coverage, not as Month-1 revenue.

## 2. Partnership Models

Sized to a nonprofit budget with no historical spend, EUR-denominated, and no ad-account or influencer tooling in place yet. Ordered from zero-cash to paid.

### 2.1 Gifted / gratitude-based partnership (no cash) — the default model

**Structure:** HOPE'87 provides the *content*, the *story*, and *public gratitude*; the partner provides distribution. No money changes hands.

**What HOPE'87 actually gives (this must be real, or the ask fails):**
- Ready-to-post assets from the Template A/B/C system in the content plan — the partner does zero design work.
- A named, specific story they can tell (a graduate's trajectory), which is genuinely good content for them, not an ad they're doing you a favour by posting.
- Public credit: a thank-you post on HOPE'87's channels, tagging, a "Community Partners" highlight (this maps to the **Community** highlight cover already specified in the content plan §1.3), and named credit in the impact recap.
- For organizations: a logo/name placement on the donation landing page's supporters section (Week 6+) and a formal thank-you letter on letterhead — which matters more to community-org leaders than any social metric.

**Where it works:** Tier 1 archetypes almost exclusively — community-org leaders and mosque-affiliated voices will generally be *insulted* by a fee offer, and diaspora micro-influencers under ~15k often accept gifted partnerships for a cause tied to their own heritage.

**Where it fails:** professionalised creators above ~30k followers with a media kit. Don't waste goodwill asking them to work free; go to §2.2 or drop them.

**Hard requirement:** even unpaid, **AT/DE disclosure law still applies.** Austrian and German advertising-disclosure rules require commercial-intent posts to be labelled, and Meta's own branded-content rules apply too. Every partnership post — gifted or paid — must carry a clear label (`Werbung` / `Paid partnership` tag / `#Kooperation`) and, where the paid-partnership tag is available, use it. This is not optional politeness; unlabelled appeals create legal exposure for a nonprofit whose entire asset is trustworthiness. Put it in the one-page agreement (Week 6).

### 2.2 Paid micro-influencer posts — surgical, capped, and only after a proof case

**Structure:** a flat fee per deliverable set, no performance component, no long tail.

**Realistic AT/DE micro-influencer rates for a nonprofit ask** (these are planning ranges to negotiate against, not quotes; nonprofits routinely get 30–50% off standard commercial rates when the ask is framed as cause work):
- 3k–10k followers: €50–150 per post + story set
- 10k–25k: €150–400
- 25k–60k: €400–900
- 60k+ / established food-culture creators: €900+ — generally out of scope for the first 90 days

**Recommended Month-1–3 posture:** hold a small **test budget of roughly €500–1,000 total** across the whole quarter for 2–4 paid placements, deployed only after at least one gifted partnership has produced a measurable result worth replicating. Do not commit paid influencer money before the Week-11 attribution check has *something* to read.

**Budget-sequencing note:** the roadmap already caps Month-1 paid ads at €20–30/day and Month-2 at ~$50/day. Influencer fees compete with that same pot. Recommendation: **paid influencer spend stays below 15% of total paid budget in the first 90 days** — the ads channel needs the data more than the influencer channel needs the cash, and gifted partnerships are genuinely viable in this community.

**Also worth more than cash to some creators:** covering travel/production costs for a program-site visit (§2.4), which is often cheaper than a fee and produces far better content.

### 2.3 "Ambassador for a cause" — ongoing relationship, 3–6 partners maximum

**Structure:** a named, ongoing relationship (initial term: 3 months, renewable at the Week-12 decision point) with a defined light commitment rather than one-off posts.

**Commitment shape (keep it small enough that it's actually honoured):**
- 1 post + 1 story set per month (2 in a seasonal appeal month)
- Reshare HOPE'87's monthly Program-Spotlight post
- Attend or promote one community moment per quarter
- Optionally: a named fundraising goal ("help me fund 5 training places")

**What the ambassador gets:** the title and a co-branded ambassador badge/asset from the rebrand kit, early access to graduate stories, a direct line to a named HOPE'87 contact (not a generic inbox), inclusion in the quarterly impact report by name, and — the strongest incentive for this cohort — a **specific, attributable outcome they can point to**: "the training places my followers funded."

**Why this is the highest-leverage model for HOPE'87 specifically:** the voice is warm/community-rooted and the CTA style is soft. A soft CTA needs *repetition and familiarity* to convert — a single post from a stranger doesn't get there, but the fifth post from a familiar ambassador does. Ongoing beats one-off for this exact voice profile.

**Cap it at 3–6 ambassadors.** More than that and nobody gets real relationship management, which is the entire product being offered. The roadmap's Week-6 "3–5 influencers/orgs onboarded" is the right number — hold that line.

### 2.4 Content swap — program-site visit or graduate interview

**Structure:** HOPE'87 provides access and story; the partner produces content they own and publish. No fee, sometimes a travel/production cost contribution.

**Two versions, at very different cost points:**

- **Version A (low cost, do this first): remote graduate interview.** A 20–30 minute video call between an AT/DE-based creator and a program graduate, with HOPE'87 handling introductions, consent, and translation. The creator edits it into their own format. Cost: coordination time only. This is realistic inside 90 days and should be the default content-swap offer.

- **Version B (high cost, high payoff, plan for Month 4+): physical program-site visit.** A creator travels to a program location, films the training in progress, and returns with a documentary-grade series. Costs: flights, accommodation, local fixer, safeguarding/consent process. **Do not promise this inside the first 90 days** — it needs budget approval, a safeguarding review of filming beneficiaries, and it competes directly with the Section-3 video shoot already scoped in the content plan. Flag it as a Q4/Month-4+ option in the Week-12 renewal conversation.

**Alignment note:** the content plan's video shoot (§3.2) already schedules graduate and trainer interviews. **Reuse that shoot for influencer content swaps rather than commissioning separately** — a creator can narrate over existing b-roll (see format §3.1), which costs nothing extra and keeps the visual language consistent with the rebrand kit.

**Safeguarding, non-negotiable:** any content featuring a program beneficiary requires documented informed consent for the specific platforms and uses, no financial-hardship framing that strips dignity (the content plan's "what can you do now, not how poor were you" rule applies to influencers too), and no location details that could put a participant at risk. Write these into the agreement; influencers will not know these rules unprompted.

## 3. Content Collaboration Formats

Three formats, chosen because each is producible from assets **already scoped** in the content plan, and each fits a different point in the funnel. All must use the rebrand kit templates and the shared footer handle/contact bar so co-branded content is visually continuous with HOPE'87's own feed.

### Format 1 — "Meet [Name]": influencer narrates a graduate's story
*Funnel role: trust + top-of-funnel reach. The default format — start every partnership here.*

**Mechanic:** HOPE'87 supplies b-roll and stills from the video shoot (graduate practising their trained skill) plus a factual one-pager. The influencer records their **own voiceover in their own language and register** — Urdu, Punjabi, German, or code-switched — telling the graduate's arc: what they couldn't do, what the training gave them, what they do now. 30–60 seconds, Reels/Feed-native, 9:16 cut already specified in the content plan (§2, "Story-format vertical cuts").

**Why it works for this audience:** it converts the influencer from advertiser to *narrator* — they lend voice and credibility without claiming personal expertise about the NGO. It also solves the bilingual problem elegantly: the same footage yields Urdu, German, and English versions at zero incremental production cost, matching the content plan's "one design, two language exports" principle.

**Deliverables per partner:** 1 Reel + 3 Stories + 1 static Template-A quote card reshare.
**CTA:** soft, per the voice rule — "the link in HOPE'87's bio shows how the training works" (pre-Week 6) or a direct donation-page link (post-Week 6).
**Constraint:** requires the video shoot footage to exist. Realistically this means **first co-branded posts land Week 8**, exactly as the roadmap has it — not earlier.

### Format 2 — "Match my donation" challenge
*Funnel role: conversion + the closest thing to trackable attribution available. Deploy at a seasonal moment.*

**Mechanic:** the influencer publicly commits a personal amount (e.g. €200 — or, better, HOPE'87 or a single major donor pledges the match so the creator risks nothing) and challenges their audience to match it toward a **specific, countable unit**: training places, sewing machines, tool kits. Progress is posted daily in Stories for 5–7 days. Closes with the total and a thank-you naming the outcome funded ("14 training places").

**Why it works here:** it converts a vague donation ask into a concrete, culturally familiar collective-giving mechanic — structurally the same as a community fundraising drive at an event, which this donor base already understands. It also creates the only genuinely legible attribution signal available pre-tooling: a defined window, a defined target, a countable result.

**Why "training places" and not euros in the copy:** aligns with the cause focus and the "storytelling over statistics" voice rule — the number is a *unit of human outcome*, not a fundraising thermometer.

**Attribution mechanic (see §5):** a per-influencer donation landing-page slug (e.g. `hope87.at/de/spenden/[name]`) plus a "How did you hear about us?" field on the donation form.
**Timing:** requires the donation landing page (Week 6). **Do not run a match challenge before Week 6** — sending challenge traffic to an untracked PayPal link wastes the one format that could have been measured. Best window: Week 9–11, so results feed the Week-11 attribution check.

### Format 3 — Instagram Story takeover during a fundraising moment
*Funnel role: mid-funnel warmth + net-new followers for HOPE'87's own account. Highest value against the 289-follower problem.*

**Mechanic:** for one day during a defined moment (a seasonal appeal, a program milestone, a community event), the influencer runs 5–8 sequential Stories on **their own** account — not HOPE'87's — walking their audience through the cause: intro to why they care, the graduate's story, a poll or question sticker ("what skill would you want to learn?"), the ask, and a close. Every frame carries the HOPE'87 handle sticker and a link sticker.

**Direction matters:** takeovers should run **on the influencer's account, pointing at HOPE'87** — the reverse (influencer posting to HOPE'87's Stories) reaches 289 people and is nearly pointless right now. Revisit the reverse direction once HOPE'87's own following is materially larger.

**Why Stories:** interactive stickers (poll, question, quiz) generate the engagement signal that boosts subsequent reach, and question-sticker replies produce a small pool of **real audience language** worth harvesting for ad copy and DE-language creative. Stories also feel personal and unpolished, which suits a warm/humble voice better than a produced ad.

**Deliverables per partner:** 5–8 Stories + 1 permanent highlight save (so the content doesn't evaporate in 24 hours) + a follow-up feed post if the Story performs.
**Measurable now, even pre-Pixel:** the influencer screenshots their Story insights (reach, link taps, sticker interactions); HOPE'87 measures its own follower delta and profile-visit spike on the takeover day. Against a 289-follower baseline, a takeover's follower effect is unusually legible — a small base is, for once, an advantage.

**One format explicitly not recommended in the first 90 days:** giveaway/prize mechanics. They inflate follower counts with prize-hunters, attract non-donor audiences, dilute a nonprofit's tone, and would contaminate the very metrics (follower growth, engagement rate) being used as attribution proxies while real tracking is absent.

## 4. Sourcing Approach

Practical, low-cost, no paid influencer-discovery tooling required. Target output per the roadmap: **15–20 longlist entries by end of Week 2**, shortlist of 5–8 by Week 4, 3–5 signed by Week 6.

### 4.1 Hashtag + geo search patterns on Instagram (highest yield per hour)

Search these directly, then mine the follower/following graphs and comment sections of anything relevant — the neighbours of a good account are usually also good accounts.

**Identity + geography hashtags (start here):**
`#pakistanisinaustria` `#pakistaniinvienna` `#pakistaniwien` `#pakistanisingermany` `#pakistaniingermany`
`#desiinvienna` `#desiingermany` `#desiineurope` `#pakistanidiaspora`
`#pakistanigermany` `#urduvlogger` `#urdugermany` `#paknews_austria` — plus German-language variants
`#pakistanerinösterreich` `#pakistanerindeutschland`

**Vertical + community hashtags:**
`#desifoodvienna` `#pakistanifoodberlin` `#halalvienna` `#halalfoodfrankfurt` `#pakistaniweddinggermany` `#desiwedding europe` `#modestfashiongermany`

**Location-tag mining (often better than hashtags):** search Instagram *places*, not tags, for Pakistani restaurants, halal butchers and grocery stores, Urdu bookshops, community centres, and event venues in Vienna (especially the 10th/15th/16th districts), Graz, Linz, and in Germany across Frankfurt/Offenbach, Hamburg, Berlin-Neukölln/Wedding, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Munich, and the Ruhr cities. **The people who repeatedly geo-tag these places and get real comments are the community micro-influencers.** This surfaces genuinely local accounts and filters out Pakistan-based accounts that hashtag searches drag in.

**Event mining:** search for Pakistan Independence Day (14 August — note this falls in **Week 2**, exactly when the longlist is being built, which is fortunate timing), Eid community events, mushaira/poetry evenings, cricket-club matches, and Pakistani-association galas in AT/DE. Whoever posts the most coverage of these events is, by definition, a connected community voice.

**Qualification pass — apply before adding to the longlist:**
1. Actually resident in AT/DE? (check bio, geo-tags, language of Stories)
2. Audience 25–55 and AT/DE-based? (request an insights screenshot before any agreement — never assume)
3. Real engagement? (comment quality, not rate alone; 2%+ engagement on a 5k account with conversational comments beats 0.4% on 50k)
4. Brand-safe? (no gambling/crypto/get-rich sponsorships, no partisan political content, no prior public fundraising disputes)
5. Plausibly cause-motivated? (any prior charity resharing, community-volunteering, or Pakistan-related civic content is a strong yes-signal)

### 4.2 Community organization referrals (the highest-conversion source)

**The single most efficient sourcing move available:** get one Tier-1 community-organization leader to say yes, then ask them — in the same conversation — for two names. Diaspora community networks are dense and referral-based; a warm intro from a respected chairperson converts at a dramatically higher rate than a cold DM, and it pre-vets brand-safety for you.

**Practical steps:**
- Map the associations first: Pakistani associations and cultural societies in Vienna, Graz, Linz; Pakistani-German associations and business/professional networks in the German cluster cities; Pakistani student associations at Vienna, Graz, Frankfurt, Berlin, and Munich universities (these are also the pipeline for archetype 1.5).
- Check whether the Pakistani embassy/consulate community pages in Vienna and Berlin list registered community organizations — this is a free, credible directory.
- Ask every yes for two referrals. **Build the referral ask into the outreach template**, so it happens by default rather than when someone remembers.

**Also check HOPE'87's own institutional network first, before any cold outreach:** an NGO established in 1987 with a Pakistan program almost certainly has existing relationships — board members, long-time donors, Austrian partner NGOs, past event attendees, honorary supporters. **Week 3, before a single cold DM: ask the client for the list of people who already know and vouch for HOPE'87.** The cheapest partner is one who is already a supporter. This is the fastest path to the Week-6 target of 3–5 signed partners.

### 4.3 Mosque and community-centre outreach (client-led)

- Identify Urdu-khutbah mosques and Pakistani-run community centres in the cluster cities. Prioritise those already running charitable collections — they have the mechanism and the habit.
- **Approach in person, not by DM.** A HOPE'87 representative attends, is introduced, and asks. Expect 3–6 weeks and committee approval.
- Bring physical credibility material: printed one-pager on the skills programs, financial-transparency summary, and photos. This audience assesses legitimacy through documents and in-person presence, not through an Instagram grid.
- **Re-read the denominational-neutrality caveat in §1.3 before sequencing outreach.** The client decides the posture; the agency does not choose by accident through outreach order.
- Ask the client directly: does HOPE'87 already have relationships with any AT/DE mosque or community centre? If yes, that is Week-3 work, not Week-8 work.

### 4.4 Mine HOPE'87's own existing following (free, already-warm, most underused)

2,696 Facebook likes and 289 Instagram followers is a small audience but a **pre-qualified** one — these people already opted in. There are almost certainly connected community voices inside it.

**Concretely, in Week 2:**
- Export/review the FB Page's most-engaged followers — the audit already flagged 18 people "talking about this Page." **Those 18 are a starting shortlist**, not a footnote. Check each profile: are any of them community-org affiliated, event organisers, or creators?
- Review who reshares HOPE'87 posts, who comments substantively (not just reactions), and who tags others in comments — tagging behaviour is the clearest marker of a community connector.
- Check the 289 IG followers manually. At that size it's a 30-minute job, and it's the highest-density source of pre-warmed candidates available. Look for accounts with 2k+ followers and diaspora-relevant content.
- Review Page message history and past event attendees for engaged supporters.
- Post a soft public call on FB/IG once the rebrand is live: "we're looking for community partners in Austria and Germany to help share our graduates' stories — DM us." Low yield in raw numbers, but self-selecting for motivation, and it costs one post.

**Tracking hygiene:** keep the longlist as a simple structured file (`clients/hope87/influencers.json` or a shared sheet) with handle, platform, archetype tier, city, follower count, engagement read, source (hashtag/referral/own-following), qualification notes, outreach status and date, and partnership model proposed. The Week-11 attribution check and Week-12 renewal decision both depend on this having been maintained from Week 2 — retrofitting it in Week 11 will not work.

## 5. Measurement

**State the honest position first, in writing, to the client:** until the donation landing page and Pixel/CAPI ship in **Week 6**, HOPE'87 **cannot attribute a single donation to a single influencer**. The audit is unambiguous — no Pixel, no GTM, no GA4 anywhere on hope87.at, and donations route through an untracked PayPal link. Any influencer-attribution claim made before Week 6 would be a guess. Say this up front rather than producing a Week-4 report full of numbers that imply causation that cannot be established.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 3–6: proxies only, explicitly labelled as proxies

| What to track | How | Honest limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Influencer post reach / impressions | Partner sends an insights screenshot 48h after posting | Self-reported; unverifiable |
| Saves + shares on partner posts | Same screenshot | The most meaningful engagement signal for cause content — shares indicate willingness to vouch |
| Story link taps / sticker interactions | Same screenshot | Taps ≠ visits; no landing-side confirmation exists yet |
| **HOPE'87 IG follower delta** | Daily manual count from the 289 baseline | Genuinely legible at this size — a 30-follower day is unmissable. Cannot separate influencer effect from the concurrent Month-1 awareness ads |
| **FB Page "talking about this"** | vs. the audit's baseline of 18 | Coarse, but a real baseline exists |
| Profile visits + reach on HOPE'87's own accounts | Native IG/FB insights, per-day | Confounded by paid + organic cadence recovery running at the same time |
| Inbound DMs / comments mentioning a partner | Manual log | Small numbers, but qualitatively the strongest early signal — someone naming the referrer is as close to attribution as Phase 1 gets |

**Phase-1 reporting rule:** report these as **activity and audience-growth metrics**, never as donation performance. The correct Week-4 sentence is "three partners delivered X reach and HOPE'87 gained Y followers; donation impact is not yet measurable and will not be until Week 6."

### Phase 2 — Week 6 onward: real attribution, three cheap mechanisms

Once the Astro donation landing page and Pixel/CAPI are live and verified (roadmap Weeks 6–7, with the fail-closed 3–5-days-of-clean-event-data gate before spend):

1. **Per-influencer UTM links.** Every partner gets a unique link following the client CLAUDE.md UTM convention:
   `https://hope87.at/de/spenden?utm_source=influencer&utm_medium=social_organic&utm_campaign=influencer_q3_2026&utm_content=[handle]`
   Note `utm_medium=social_organic` deliberately — this must not be double-counted as paid social in the day-90 report.
   **Known leakage, state it:** Instagram bio/Story links are frequently copy-pasted, screenshotted, or retyped, and iOS in-app-browser behaviour plus EU consent-banner declines will suppress a meaningful share of hits. Treat UTM numbers as a **floor, not a total**.

2. **Per-influencer landing-page slug (the promo-code equivalent).** A vanity path per partner — `hope87.at/de/spenden/aisha` — resolving to the donation page with the UTM pre-applied and, ideally, a personalised line ("Aisha asked us to share this"). This is the nonprofit substitute for a discount code: it survives copy-paste, it is memorable enough to say out loud at a community event or from a mosque announcement, and it lifts conversion by carrying the endorsement onto the page. **Cheap to implement in Astro (a static route per partner) — request it in the Week-6 build, not as an afterthought.** For Tier-1 and mosque partners whose audience is partly offline, pair the slug with a printable QR code.

3. **"How did you hear about us?" field on the donation form.** One optional dropdown/free-text field. Unglamorous and the single most reliable attribution instrument a small nonprofit has, because it captures the offline and word-of-mouth paths that no pixel can see — the mosque announcement, the WhatsApp forward, the friend who mentioned it. Include an "a community member / social media page" option with a free-text follow-up. **Add this to the Week-6 donation-page spec.**

**Then, from Week 7+, the trackable set becomes:** sessions and donation-page views by `utm_content`, `Donate` conversion events attributed to the influencer source, donation value and average gift per partner, conversion rate by partner (visits → completed donations), and cost-per-euro-raised for the small number of paid placements.

### What will never be attributable — say so plainly

- **View-through influence.** Someone sees a Story, doesn't tap, donates a week later via search or the FB Page. Real, common, invisible. Likely the *largest* share of influencer impact and permanently unmeasured.
- **Bank-transfer and offline donations.** If any AT/DE donations arrive by bank transfer or cash at a community event, the pixel never sees them. The "how did you hear about us" field is the only capture mechanism — and only if it exists on the transfer instructions too.
- **Mosque and community-event announcements.** Inherently offline. Use QR codes with per-partner slugs to recover some of it; accept that the rest is unmeasured.
- **Separating influencer effect from the concurrent paid ramp.** Month 2–3 runs influencer content, awareness ads, a conversion campaign, a site relaunch, and a rebrand simultaneously. Clean isolation of the influencer channel is not possible at this scale without a proper holdout test, which €20–50/day budgets cannot support. **Do not claim it in the day-90 report.** Report the channel's directly-tracked contribution as a floor plus a qualitative read.

### Week-11 mid-point attribution check (per roadmap) — deliverable definition

To keep the Week-12 renew/expand decision from being a vibe:
- Per-partner table: posts delivered vs. promised, reach, UTM sessions, donation-page views, tracked donations and value, "heard about us" mentions.
- **Tier and archetype rollup** — this is the strategically important output. Which archetype actually moved money? If Tier 1 community-org voices out-convert the larger food/culture creators (the expectation), Month 4+ should shift effort toward organizations and away from reach-buying.
- Cost side: fees paid, staff hours spent per partner. A gifted partner consuming 6 hours of coordination for one post is not free.
- Explicit recommendation per partner: renew / renew-with-changes / thank-and-close.

## 6. Phased Rollout (First 90 Days)

Mapped onto the roadmap's existing influencer weeks. Two gates govern the whole sequence, and both are already in the roadmap — this plan just states the influencer-side consequence.

> **Gate A — no outreach before there is a credible destination.** A community leader who is asked to vouch for HOPE'87 will immediately open the Instagram profile. If it shows 289 followers, an inconsistent logo crop, and a last post 11 days old, the ask fails and that relationship is hard to reopen. **This creates a real tension with the roadmap's Week-3 outreach start**, since the full social rebrand rollout isn't scheduled until Month 2 (Weeks 5–8). **Resolution: pull the minimum credible surface forward into Week 3** — refreshed profile picture, new FB cover, and three weeks of consistent 3x/week posting live on both accounts (all of which Weeks 1–3 already deliver). The remaining rollout items (highlight covers, full template deployment) can land in Month 2 as planned. If even that minimum is not live by Week 3, **delay Tier-1 outreach by one week rather than burning the relationship** — Tier-1 partners are not replaceable.
>
> **Gate B — no attribution promises before Week 6.** Do not tell a partner "we'll show you exactly how much your post raised" before the donation landing page and Pixel/CAPI are live and verified. Promise reach and gratitude in Weeks 3–5; promise measured impact only from Week 7. Over-promising attribution to a community leader and then failing to report it costs more trust than never having offered it.

### What this plan deliberately does NOT promise

- **Not a large-follower "influencer campaign."** The budget doesn't support it and the trust economics of this donor base don't reward it.
- **Not attributable donation revenue in Month 1.** Structurally impossible — no tracking, no donation page, no assets. Anyone reporting it would be making it up.
- **Not a program-site influencer visit inside 90 days.** Needs budget approval and a safeguarding review; it is a Month-4+ conversation.
- **Not clean channel isolation in the day-90 report.** Too many concurrent changes at this spend level. The influencer channel's tracked contribution is reported as a floor plus a qualitative read, not as a precise share.
- **Not mosque-network partnerships as an agency deliverable.** These are client-led, in-person, committee-approved relationships. The agency prepares materials and tracking; the client carries the relationship.

## Open questions for the client (blocking or shaping the above)

1. **Tax-deductibility in AT/DE.** Are HOPE'87 donations tax-deductible for Austrian and/or German donors (Austrian *Spendenabsetzbarkeit* / German *Zuwendungsbescheinigung*)? This is one of the strongest trust levers for a 25–55 diaspora donor and partners *will* be asked. If yes, it belongs in every partner content pack and on the donation page. If no, agree the honest answer now rather than improvising it in a partner's comment section.
2. **Denominational posture** for mosque/community-centre outreach (§1.3) — institutional neutrality or a chosen network. Client decision, needed before Week 3 sequencing.
3. **Existing network list** — board, long-standing AT/DE donors, partner organizations, past event collaborators. Fastest route to the Week-6 target of 3–5 signed partners.
4. **Match-funding capacity** — is there a major donor or reserve able to underwrite a €200–500 match for Format 2? A match the creator doesn't personally fund is far easier to say yes to.
5. **Influencer budget ceiling for Q1** — confirm the ~€500–1,000 test range, and confirm it comes from a separate line rather than cannibalising the €20–50/day ads budget.
6. **Named relationship owner at HOPE'87.** Ambassadors were promised "a direct line to a named contact" (§2.3). Who is it? Community partners will not accept a shared inbox.
7. **Safeguarding policy for beneficiary content** — does HOPE'87 have an existing written consent/safeguarding policy for filming and publishing program participants? If yes, it governs all influencer content. If no, one is needed before Week 8.
